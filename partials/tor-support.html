Protect your IP

Vexec stores nothing about you. But your internet service provider can still see that you visit this site. Here's how to make that impossible.

5-minute read Works everywhere Free & open-source tools

When you connect to any website, your device sends its IP address along with the request. This is how the internet works — there's no way to connect without one.

Vexec doesn't store your IP. Our servers receive it, process your request, and forget it. But your ISP (the company that provides your internet) also sees every domain you visit. They can't see what you read or write on Vexec — because everything is encrypted — but they can see that you were here.

If you want to hide that too, there are three ways. Each has trade-offs. Pick the one that matches your threat model.

Three ways to hide your IP

Best

Tor

Routes your traffic through three random relays. Nobody knows both who you are and what you're doing.

  • ✅ Hides IP from us and your ISP
  • ✅ Free, open source
  • ✅ Recommended by journalists, activists
  • ⚠️ Slightly slower
Easiest

VPN

Encrypts your traffic to a single provider, who sees your real IP and forwards the traffic on your behalf.

  • ✅ Hides IP from us and your ISP
  • ✅ Faster than Tor
  • ⚠️ The VPN company knows your IP
  • ⚠️ Paid ones are usually more trustworthy
Most private

Self-host

Run your own copy of Vexec on a server you control. No third party ever sees your traffic.

  • ✅ Zero trust required in anyone
  • ✅ Full control
  • ⚠️ Requires technical setup
  • ⚠️ You pay for the server

How to use Tor

Tor is a network that bounces your traffic through three random computers around the world before it reaches its destination. No single relay knows both who you are and where you're going.

It's completely free, run by a nonprofit, and used by journalists, activists, and ordinary people who just want privacy.

1

Download Tor Browser

Go to torproject.org and download the Tor Browser for your operating system. It's a modified version of Firefox that routes everything through Tor automatically.

torproject.org/download
2

Install and open it

Follow the installer for your OS. No account needed. When you open Tor Browser, it will connect to the Tor network automatically (this may take 10–30 seconds).

3

Visit Vexec's .onion address

Once connected, enter our .onion address in the Tor Browser address bar. This is a special address that only works inside the Tor network — your ISP can't even see it exists.

vexec7x2k4m.onion

Replace the placeholder above with our real .onion address once it's live.

4

Log in normally

Use your 20-character access code exactly as you would on the regular site. Everything works the same — the only difference is that your IP address is now completely hidden from us, your ISP, and everyone else.

Important: don't log into the same Vexec account on both the regular site and Tor at the same time. If someone is watching, they could link your two sessions together. Pick one and stay with it.

How to use a VPN

A VPN (Virtual Private Network) encrypts all your traffic and sends it through a server run by the VPN company. Your ISP only sees that you're connected to the VPN — not which sites you visit.

The VPN company itself can see your real IP. So the question becomes: do you trust your VPN provider?

Good signs

  • Audited by third parties
  • No-logs policy (proven in court)
  • Open-source clients
  • Payments accepted in crypto
  • Based in privacy-friendly countries

Red flags

  • "Free forever" VPNs
  • No privacy policy at all
  • Collects device identifiers
  • Owned by ad companies
  • Based in surveillance-heavy countries

If you can't decide which VPN to trust, use Tor instead. Tor's architecture doesn't require trusting any single party.

How to self-host Vexec

If you don't want to trust anyone — not us, not Tor, not a VPN — you can run Vexec entirely on your own server.

The frontend is fully open source. The backend is not, but its architecture and protocol are documented. If you're comfortable with servers, you can stand up your own instance in an hour.

bash 
git clone https://github.com/vexec/client.git
cd client
python3 -m http.server 8080

This runs the frontend locally. To make it usable by others, you'd need to also implement the backend API (documented at vexec.github.io/information) on your own server. Any language works — Go, Python, Rust, Node.js.

Self-hosting means you don't have to trust anyone. The trade-off is that you're now responsible for your own security, uptime, and legal compliance.

How to verify it's working

Whichever method you use, you should confirm that your IP is actually hidden. Here's how:

1

Check your IP first (without protection)

Visit any "what is my IP" site and write down the address you see. This is what the world sees about you right now.

2

Turn on your protection

Open Tor Browser, or turn on your VPN, or switch to your self-hosted instance.

3

Check the IP again

Visit the same IP site through your protection. The address should be completely different — and often in a different country.

4

Test with Tor's own checker

The Tor Project maintains a page that specifically checks for leaks: check.torproject.org. If you're using Tor correctly, it will say so.

Frequently asked questions

Is Tor legal in my country?

In most countries, yes. Tor is used by journalists, security researchers, and ordinary people worldwide. A few countries restrict or block it. Check your local laws if you're unsure.

Does Tor slow down Vexec?

Slightly. Tor routes your traffic through three random computers, so there's a small delay (usually 200–500ms). For a text-based platform like Vexec, you probably won't notice.

Can I use Tor on my phone?

Yes. On Android, install Tor Browser or Orbot from the Play Store (or F-Droid). On iOS, install Onion Browser from the App Store. Both are free.

Do I need a VPN and Tor?

No, and combining them can actually weaken your anonymity if done wrong. Pick one. Tor alone is stronger than most VPNs for anonymity purposes.

Will you ever see my real IP?

If you connect through Tor, no. If you connect through a VPN, no — we'd see the VPN's IP, not yours. If you connect directly, we see your IP in memory (like every website does) but we never write it to disk.

What if my government blocks Tor?

Tor has built-in bridges and pluggable transports designed to bypass censorship. Look for "Bridges" in Tor Browser's settings, or use Snowflake — a browser extension that helps people connect in censored regions.

Does using Tor make me look suspicious to Vexec?

No. We don't block, flag, or treat Tor users differently. We don't log IPs at all, so we can't even tell who's using Tor and who isn't.

Take one step today

You don't have to do all three. Just pick the one that fits how much you care about this and how much effort you're willing to put in.
